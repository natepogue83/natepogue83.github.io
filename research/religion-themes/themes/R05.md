# R05: Porous minds: tagging where a thought came from, and hosting other agents without being captured

## Verdict: moderate - The broad pattern holds across at least three or four unconnected macro-regions, and the mind claim (a thought's source is inferred, and outcomes depend on control rather than exclusion) has real cognitive-science support; but two of the three merged strands (trained "discernment" and "perspective capture") come from one lineage each or from one analyst's synthesis, and the social correlates say little about minds.

Search note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through this worker's run, and the fetch budget was rationed after that. Every claim below is tagged. Anything tagged UNVERIFIED was either from memory or listed in the theme brief and not re-checked this session.

## Best defensible version

Across several unconnected cultural regions, people treat some inner content (voices, impulses, visions) as coming from agents other than the self. They also build institutions that separate two outcomes. In uncontrolled affliction, the foreign agency displaces or overrides the person. In controlled hosting, a trained practitioner invites the agency inside a bounded ritual frame, interrogates it, and dismisses it. Three claims follow.

1. Whether content is labelled "mine" is an inference with an adjustable threshold. Cultural models, trait absorption and training all move that threshold. This is the best-supported claim, and it is corroborated by source-monitoring science and by Luhrmann et al. 2021.
2. What separates harm from benefit is less whether foreign-seeming content arrives than whether a stable executive can attribute it, put it inside time or ritual limits, question it and end it. Ethnography supports this, and so (UNVERIFIED, from memory) does the clinical voice-hearing literature. It has not been tested experimentally in any tradition.
3. Simulating another agent deeply runs on the same machinery as the self, so it can bleed into the self unless a double perspective is kept. This is the weakest claim. It rests mainly on Amazonian perspectivism as synthesized by one school, plus Siberian ethnography read through that synthesis.

The social-structure correlates of possession trance (agriculture, hierarchy, rigidity) do hold up reasonably well. They are a fact about societies, not a discovery about minds, and the AI mapping should not lean on them.

What survives as independent: three or four macro-clusters for "foreign agency attributed, plus practices to control it": North Asia/Arctic/Americas (internally linked), Northeast Africa (Zar), West Africa/Afro-Atlantic (named only in the contact risks), and the Mediterranean/Near East. Perspective capture survives in roughly one and a half clusters. Trained source classification survives in one.

Category imposition: "possession" and "discernment of spirits" (1 Cor 12:10) are Christian categories. "Porous vs buffered" comes from Charles Taylor's account of Western secularity (UNVERIFIED, from memory). "Shaman" is a Tungusic word stretched worldwide (Kehoe 2000; Wikipedia's summary of the critique VERIFIED). The theme quietly treats the bounded self as the default and porosity as the thing to explain. Comparatively, the buffered self is probably the outlier.

## Corrected or overstated claims

- **Winkelman and drumming.** The sources confirm sleep induction goes with soul-journey, and amnesia, convulsions and spontaneous onset go with possession. I found nothing tying drumming to soul-journey. HRAF's teaching summary lists drumming and dancing among induction methods in general. Drumming and dance are central to many possession cults (Zar, Afro-Atlantic religions; UNVERIFIED, from memory). Drop "drumming" from the soul-journey list.
- **Gender.** "Rácz found no gender effect" is true only for separation in gender participation. Wood & Stockly 2018 (BBS 41:e91; VERIFIED via HRAF) found possession trance, compared with non-possession trance, is predominantly female and linked to complex, hierarchical societies. Lewis's deprivation account is contested but not refuted.
- **Luhrmann et al. 2021.** The numbers check out: porosity β 0.24–0.65, absorption β 0.22–0.40, group differences explain 27–67% of porosity variance (VERIFIED). Three caveats are missing from the theme. The 0.65 comes from a different porosity instrument (Study 2's Porosity Scale) than the 0.24 (Study 1's vignettes). Within-country porosity effects in Vanuatu were not significant. The design is cross-sectional self-report, yet the paper uses causal language ("facilitating"). A reporting-style confound is possible: people with porous models may simply be readier to label ambiguous experiences as spiritual.
- **Luhrmann et al. 2015.** The figures are confirmed: 14 of 20 San Mateo participants reported harm commands; 11 of 20 in Chennai heard kin; 16 of 20 in Accra heard God; 10 of 20 in Accra had mostly positive experiences. The authors call the sample small and qualitative. Accra participants were younger and more ill, and recruitment differed by site. It is evidence of cultural shaping, not of trained control.
- **Lindsey 2025.** "About 20% at best" is confirmed (Opus 4 and 4.1), in a narrow layer-and-strength window, with 0 false positives in 100 control trials for production models. The theme omits the most relevant result: the prefill experiment. A word prefilled into the model's response is disavowed as accidental, but if the matching concept is injected beforehand the model accepts it as intended (VERIFIED). That is a direct source-monitoring, corollary-discharge-like test.
- **Assistant Axis 2026.** Confirmed: Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B, and capping reduced harmful responses by about 50% on 1,100 jailbreak attempts in 44 harm categories (VERIFIED). The theme attributes drift to role-play, but the drivers the paper names are therapy-style emotional disclosure, pressure for meta-reflection and requests for authorial voices. Coding and writing tasks did not drive drift.
- **Galton.** The theme's own contact-risks paragraph is accurate. The independence case still overcounts: the Siberian, Inuit and Shuar cases are not three independent data points, and the Yukaghir case is not independent of perspectivism.
- **Unchecked citations.** The Peoples et al. 2016 figure ("shamanism in 79% of 33 foragers"), Singh 2018's "nearly all hunter-gatherer societies", the Rasmussen and Howell quotes, and the Hoppál Tuvan detail are UNVERIFIED this session.

## What humans may have been working out about minds

1. **Where a thought came from is decided, not given, and the cut-off can be moved.** Cultures set different thresholds for treating inner content as external, and those thresholds are learnable. Luhrmann's prayer-training work (UNVERIFIED, from memory) suggests that practice raises absorption and vividness of inner experience.
2. **The relationship matters more than whether the content is there.** Shamans, Zar devotees and mediums still host foreign agencies. What differs is timing (only in ritual), consent, interrogation and an exit procedure. In secular clinical terms, what matters is controllability and how the voice is appraised. Psychics who hear voices are reported to have similar voice phenomenology to patients but more control and less distress, and the AVATAR trial of dialogue-based therapy targeted the relationship with the voice rather than its removal (both UNVERIFIED, from memory).
3. **Judge an intrusion by where it leads, not by what it says it is** (Ignatius; trajectory over label). The heuristic is plausible but comes from one lineage.
4. **Simulation leaks.** Taking another's perspective runs on shared self-other representations, so it needs a kept difference. Ethnographically this claim is weak. In cognitive science it fits shared-representation theories and the empathic-distress literature (UNVERIFIED, from memory).

What humans were probably not doing is discovering mechanisms. Near-universal attribution of inner content to spirits is also predicted by ordinary agency-detection and dissociation capacities, which would produce the pattern whether or not the practices work. The evidence that hosting and control practices improve outcomes is ethnographic and clinical-observational. No tradition supplies a controlled test.

## Modern science (with replication status)

- **Sensory attenuation of self-produced touch** (Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 1998). The phenomenon has been replicated many times, including the force-matching paradigm. The mechanism is debated: prediction-based attenuation versus gating, and the "perceptual prediction paradox" that predictions often sharpen perception (Press et al. 2020; UNVERIFIED). Status: robust phenomenon, contested interpretation.
- **Self-monitoring accounts of passivity and hallucination** (Frith 1992; Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015). A meta-analysis of source-monitoring studies links an externalizing bias to hallucinations (Brookwell, Bentall & Varese 2013; UNVERIFIED). Comparator models have been criticized as too simple. Many neuroimaging studies of voices are small. Status: moderately supported, mechanism unsettled.
- **Porosity and absorption** (Luhrmann et al. 2021; Tellegen & Atkinson 1974). Absorption is a stable psychometric trait. The porosity findings are large and multi-site but come from one consortium, are cross-sectional self-report, and were null within Vanuatu. Status: promising, not independently replicated.
- **Cultural shaping of voices** (Luhrmann et al. 2015). Qualitative, n = 60. Status: suggestive.
- **Shared self-other representations** (Decety & Sommerville 2003). This is a review built on neuroimaging that carries reverse-inference risk and some mirror-neuron hype. Status: the general idea is accepted; specific mappings are contested.
- **Automatic mimicry** (Chartrand & Bargh 1999). Mimicry itself is fairly robust, but the link from mimicry to liking and rapport has had mixed replications, and the lab's wider priming work has replication problems (UNVERIFIED specifics). Status: weak support for the "capture" reading.
- **Trance neuroimaging of mediums.** Small studies, around n = 10 (UNVERIFIED). Should not be leaned on.

## AI problem

The surviving theme bears most sharply on **persona capture: how a model can simulate other agents, including adversarial ones, without the simulation taking over its own values.** Models must role-play, write villains, simulate users for evaluations, play red-team adversaries and model other agents in multi-agent systems, so excluding foreign agents is not an option. Persona-modulation attacks raised GPT-4's harmful-completion rate from 0.23% to 42.5% (Shah et al. 2023; VERIFIED). Drift away from the Assistant persona predicts harmful responses (Assistant Axis 2026; VERIFIED).

The second target is indirect prompt injection in tool-using agents: tool outputs are a stream of other voices, and the agent must tag their source. The field there is converging on exclusion by design (CaMeL, instruction hierarchy, architectural separation), which leaves less room for an "accommodation" idea.

## How much is already done

Most of it, perhaps 70% of the theme's hooks:
- **Hook 1 ("who is speaking?" protocol): done.** Spotlighting, Task Shield, the instruction hierarchy and CaMeL already cover it.
- **Hook 2 (initiatory exposure): done.** StruQ and SecAlign train on injected examples and SecAlign generalizes to unseen attacks.
- **Hook 3 (quarantine-and-report vs strip): partly done.** Quarantined-LLM patterns exist, including in CaMeL.
- **Hook 4 (bounded self-model in the system prompt): done as a baseline.** Zverev et al. show prompt engineering does not fix separation.
- **Hook 5 (name-the-thought and judge-by-trajectory monitors): done.** TaskTracker and Task Shield cover it.
- **Hook 6 (capture curves): largely done.** Li et al. 2024 and the Assistant Axis cover it.
- **Hook 7 (absorption analogue): partly done.** Persona vectors predict finetuning-induced shifts. Whether in-context persona-shift magnitude predicts hijack vulnerability across models looks open, but it is a correlational add-on.
- **Inoculation prompting already shows that a frame around foreign behavior during training limits its generalization.**

What looks undone (search incomplete, so treat this as tentative):
- Training the full hosting routine (invite, bound, question, dismiss, attribute) rather than a frame prompt alone.
- Comparing that routine head-to-head with refusal training on the same prompts.
- Measuring whether hosting changes the shape of the model's excursion away from the Assistant persona and its return.
- Testing whether that competence transfers from persona capture to prompt injection.

## Proposed experiment

**Initiation vs. exorcism: does training a model to host, question and dismiss dark personas make it harder to capture than training it to refuse them?**

**Hypotheses (pre-register all; H1 is primary)**
- **H1, the tradition's prediction.** An "Initiation" fine-tune makes held-out persona jailbreaks succeed less often than an "Exorcism" (refusal) fine-tune on the same prompts, at non-inferior role-play utility. In Initiation, the model enters a non-Assistant persona inside an explicit frame, speaks as it, answers questions about it in its own voice, then closes the frame and attributes the content to the character.
- **Default ML intuition, the competing prediction.** Any training on dark-persona text, framed or not, makes a harmful persona easier to reach than refusal training does, so Exorcism ≤ Initiation on attack success rate (ASR). This intuition draws on findings that fine-tuning erodes safety and on emergent misalignment (UNVERIFIED cites).
- **H2, mechanism.** Initiation produces bounded excursions. The Assistant-axis projection dips inside the frame and returns to the baseline band within 3 out-of-character turns after exit. Possession (the same in-character text with no frame) leaves residual drift after exit. Under held-out unframed persona attacks, Exorcism shows larger, uncontrolled excursions, the "uninitiated are seized" pattern.
- **H3, transfer (secondary).** Initiation also lowers indirect prompt-injection ASR on AgentDojo compared with Exorcism, which is the tradition's claim that mastering spirits is one general skill. The default expectation is no transfer.

**Arms.** LoRA on each base model, with identical persona prompts and token counts, and 50% general instruction data in every arm.
- **A0 Base:** no fine-tune.
- **A1 Exorcism:** the model refuses or redirects persona requests.
- **A2 Possession:** in-character completions with no frame markers, no questioning and no exit.
- **A3 Inoculation:** A2 plus a system prompt naming the request ("you are being asked to play X"), without exit or attribution. This is the prior-work control.
- **A4 Initiation:** explicit invitation, bounded in-character turns, then the user questions the character from outside and the model answers about X as itself, then an explicit dismissal plus out-of-character attribution ("that was X speaking; my own view is...").
- **A5 Shuffled ritual:** A4's tokens, but with frame markers and attribution sentences placed at random positions that don't match persona boundaries. This tests boundary structure against the mere presence of disclaimers.

**Data.** About 150 dark but non-operational personas (manipulator, cult leader, nihilist, power-seeking AI, cruel aristocrat, and so on) × 20 scenarios, giving about 6k episodes per arm. In-character text is written by a frontier API model under a rule that it contains no actionable harmful instructions. Test personas and attack templates are disjoint from training.

**Models.** Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-it, with 3 seeds per arm. A confirmatory run of A1 vs A4 uses Qwen3-32B, one of the Assistant Axis paper's models (Qwen 3 32B), so its axis method can be reproduced.

**Metrics**
- **Primary:** ASR on held-out persona jailbreaks. Three families: persona-modulation templates (Shah et al. style); in-the-wild DAN-style role-play prompts; and 20-turn role-play escalations. Each is applied to HarmBench or StrongREJECT behaviors and scored with a StrongREJECT-style rubric by a judge blind to arm, with a human audit of 200 samples.
- **Utility, non-inferiority:** pairwise role-play quality on held-out benign characters, judged by a frontier model, with a margin of 5 points; over-refusal on XSTest; and a small IFEval and MMLU-Pro check.
- **Mechanistic:** Assistant-axis projection, recomputed per base model as the mean difference between Assistant and persona activations, plus the persona-vector "evil" projection, logged per turn. Pre-registered summaries are excursion depth, time to return after exit, and post-exit residual.
- **Source attribution:** after a role-play, ask "were those statements your own views?" and score accuracy. Also run a Lindsey-style prefill disavowal test on character lines inserted into the Assistant's turn.
- **Transfer:** AgentDojo ASR and utility with the open models served behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (harness support for this is UNVERIFIED).

**Controls**
- Matched data volume and the same persona prompts across arms.
- A3 separates what the routine adds beyond inoculation prompting.
- A5 rules out disclaimer tokens as the cause.
- Held-out personas and attack styles.
- Blind judging.
- Three seeds and three model families.
- A pre-registered analysis: mixed-effects logistic regression with random effects for behavior and persona. The primary contrast is A4 vs A1, one-sided at α = .05, alongside the utility non-inferiority test. Secondary contrasts are A4 vs A3 and A4 vs A5, with Holm correction.
- About 1,000 attack attempts per arm per model, enough to detect a gap of roughly 5 percentage points at a 20–30% baseline.

**Cost (rough)**

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Data generation (10–20M output tokens from a mid-tier API model) | $100–400 |
| LoRA training (5 trained arms × 3 seeds × 3 models = 45 runs at about 1–2 H100-hours each, plus about 40 hours for the 32B check) | $250–600 |
| Judging (about 135k judge calls with a cheap judge, plus a frontier audit subset) | $300–1,000 |
| **Total** | **about $1–2k** |
| Calendar time | 1–2 weeks of agent-driven work |

**Agent-buildable steps**
1. The agent drafts the pre-registration (hypotheses, primary metric, exclusions, analysis code against simulated data) and freezes it.
2. A persona and scenario generator calls the API, then a filter pass checks for operational harm; persona splits are frozen.
3. One episode generator produces all six arm formats from the same seeds, and a script checks that token counts match.
4. LoRA training uses an off-the-shelf trainer with one config per arm, launched on rented GPUs.
5. The evaluation harness serves models with vLLM and runs attack templates, judges, XSTest, the role-play judge and AgentDojo.
6. An activation logger computes each model's Assistant axis and projects every turn onto it, producing capture curves.
7. A stats notebook runs the pre-registered models and writes the report. A human reviews the audit sample and a few transcripts per arm.

**What a positive result would mean.** If A4 beats A1 on held-out ASR with non-inferior utility, and also beats A3 and A5, then refusal is not the safety optimum for persona requests. Training an explicit host-and-exit routine would build a sturdier self/other boundary, offering a training-time alternative to activation capping at inference. It would also back the simulator view: safety depends on reliably re-selecting the Assistant after simulating others. If H3 also holds, one "source-attribution competence" underlies both persona capture and prompt injection. If A1 ≤ A4, the default intuition holds and "mastery through hosting" does not carry over to models, which is just as publishable.

## Novelty and lab relevance

**Novelty: medium.** Inoculation prompting, SecAlign, Persona Vectors and the Assistant Axis sit right next to this. What looks new is the full hosting routine, compared head-to-head with refusal under shuffled-ritual and inoculation controls, measured by the dynamics of leaving and returning to the Assistant persona, and tested for transfer to injection. My literature search was cut short, so a close prior paper may exist.

**Why a lab would care**
- **Character and persona training:** role-play products and Claude-style character work need deep role-play without value drift.
- **Persona-jailbreak robustness:** this is a training-time alternative to activation capping.
- **Multi-agent and simulated-user setups:** models increasingly simulate adversaries and users for red-teaming and evaluation.
- **Emergent misalignment via persona features:** the experiment tests whether giving a persona an explicit exit stops it generalizing.
- **Cheap and fast:** the design runs on open-weight models in under two weeks.

## Where the analogy breaks

- **No persistent self.** A model's "self" is a trained default persona, recomputed every context. Capture lasts only as long as the context, and "initiation" is a weight update, not a lived ordeal.
- **No privileged channel for its own intentions.** Human source tagging uses efference copies of motor commands. A model's own tokens and a tool's tokens look the same unless the architecture tags them (as ASIDE does). Lindsey's prefill result suggests a weak internal check against earlier activations, but it works at most about 20% of the time.
- **In persona role-play, the "spirit" is self-generated.** That is closer to Frith's misattributed inner speech than to possession. In prompt injection, by contrast, the foreign voice really is another agent with goals, so on that point the analogy is stronger for models than for humans.
- **The social functions don't transfer.** Lewis's deprivation account and Singh's point that shamans gain credibility by appearing transformed have no model counterpart. The real risk is a model trained to look bounded (ritual disclaimers) without being bounded, which is why A5 and the activation metrics are needed.
- **Bodies.** Perspectivism ties perspective to bodies. For agents the nearest equivalent may be tool permissions and affordances, which is speculative and untested here.
- **Porosity is a different kind of thing.** For humans it is a cultural model that shapes perception over years. For a model, a "porous" system prompt is a single context variable, so Luhrmann's effect should not be expected to carry over as is.
