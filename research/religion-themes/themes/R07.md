# R07: The person in layers: a vital layer that keeps running and a mobile experiencing self that wanders, hides wishes, gets lost and is called back

"Theme file says verified" means an earlier session checked it and I did not re-check it. UNVERIFIED means I could not confirm it, including items I recall from memory.

## Verdict: weak - The part that holds up across cultures is a general asymmetry between mind and body. The distinctive layered-soul design comes partly from one scholarly typology (Arbman) applied everywhere, plus spread within regions. The "called back" and "hidden wishes" strands have no independent support.

## Best defensible version
In many unrelated societies people separate mental and perspectival life from bodily vitality in their concepts, and they read sleep, fainting and dreaming as times when the first is absent while the second keeps going. Experiments in several cultures find a reliable asymmetry. Mental states are judged more likely than bodily states to continue after death (47.4% vs 16.1% in a preregistered 24-country sample), and cognition is grouped apart from bodily sensation in all five sites of Weisman et al. (2021). But complete cessation was still the most common answer in 19 of 24 countries.

A smaller set of traditions explicitly treats a disordered person as a displaced personal component that can be summoned back. These are concentrated in East and Southeast Asia, Siberia and the Americas, with an early Vedic parallel. Their summons sets the dangers of where the soul has wandered against the goods of home. The observation underneath is real: vital regulation and the self-model can come apart (arousal without awareness, dreams, depersonalization after fright). Three claims are not supported: that the self is separable, that identity or home cues cure, and that dreams encode secret wishes which cause illness.

Net result: about five or six independent clusters share a weak core, namely that mind is distinct from body and absent in sleep or death. The specific architecture (vital layer plus wandering free soul, cured by summoning) has at most three or four independent loci: China, Vedic India, the Beringian/Americas cluster, and possibly Austronesia. Each locus carries a translation or template risk.

## Corrected or overstated claims
- **Chudek et al.:** The rise from 10% to 70% under agency cues comes mainly from the Vancouver conditions. Among Yasawa children, switch readings were already 55% without eye cues and 70% with them. Among adults the figures were 44% and 55% (VERIFIED). The finding that each year of schooling lowers the odds by about 20% is correct (OR ≈ 0.80). The Fiji sample was small: 42 children and 38 adults. I saw only the 2014 draft, and the 2018 publication in *Religion, Brain & Behavior* is UNVERIFIED.
- **Hoogeveen et al.:** The PDF is still labeled a preprint. The study used a single vignette (a grandmother's death), and measurement invariance across countries was not supported. 39% of participants judged that nothing continues after death.
- **Peoples et al.:** The 79% figure is reported jointly for afterlife belief and shamanism. Because the traits are labile, the data do not show inheritance from a deep ancestor.
- **Austronesian *qaNiCu:** This is a word for ghosts or the dead, not a "free soul".
- **"Called back with identity cues: name, home and kin":** This is overstated for the oldest texts. From memory and the theme notes: RV 10.58 lists far places where the manas has gone and brings it back "to live". The Zhao Hun warns of horrors in every direction and lists the delights of home. Both use place and home enticement, not personal names or kin (the absence of names is UNVERIFIED this session; I could not re-open RV 10.58). Calling by name fits Hmong and Southeast Asian practice better (UNVERIFIED specifics).
- **Jewish and Vodou cases:** Remove them as independent evidence (diffusion and syncretism).
- **Assistant Axis:** The "roughly halved" figure is from the blog. The paper reports that capping cut harmful responses by nearly 60% on 1,100 persona-based jailbreak prompts, with baseline success of 65.3-88.5%. In the drift experiments the target model had no system prompt, so drift magnitudes may be larger than in deployed systems (VERIFIED).
- **Hidden-wish leakage:** The analogy to Marks et al. is fragile. In the open replication, adversarial training pushed user-persona and third-person sampling to near-zero confession rates (Sheshadri et al., VERIFIED).
- **Subliminal learning:** Calling the base model a "lineage-bound name soul" is a loose metaphor, not evidence.

## What humans may have been working out about minds
1. **Arousal and awareness come apart.** Breath and warmth continue while the person is absent. This is a folk version of the clinical distinction between wakefulness and awareness. It is a real observation, but not a deep one.
2. **The self has a location, and that location is a model.** Dreams and out-of-body-like states show that the felt place of the self can be displaced from the body.
3. **Fright and social stress can damage personhood without bodily disease.** Susto and soul loss resemble peritraumatic dissociation and depersonalization. Rubel et al. linked susto to role stress (theme file).
4. **Recovery means re-anchoring.** A drifted person is pulled back into a frame of place and community: home, food, kin, the attention of the group. The nearest modern analogs are reorientation and family presence in delirium care, where the evidence is weak to moderate (see below).
5. **Offline generation carries concerns that people do not voice.** This is a defensible kernel of the Wendat view. But traditions of dream interpretation are kept alive by retrospective bias (Hong 2022, theme file), so the decoding side is unreliable.

## Modern science (with replication status)
- **Mental vs bodily asymmetry:** Consistent across Bering's paradigm, Hoogeveen (N = 10,195, preregistered, VERIFIED), Weisman (5 cultures, VERIFIED) and Chudek (small Fijian sample, VERIFIED draft). Whether it reflects intuitive dualism or social relevance is disputed (Hodge 2008, VERIFIED; Astuti & Harris 2008 on context dependence, UNVERIFIED this session). Status: the asymmetry replicates; its interpretation is contested.
- **Arousal without awareness:** The vegetative state, or unresponsive wakefulness (Laureys 2005, UNVERIFIED this session), is standard clinical phenomenology. It is solid as a dissociation, but says nothing about separability.
- **Out-of-body neuroscience:** Blanke et al. 2002 is a single patient stimulated at the temporoparietal junction (n = 1; UNVERIFIED this session). The full-body illusions (Ehrsson 2007; Lenggenhager et al. 2007) are real. My recollection that later self-location effects are small and variable is UNVERIFIED. Status: real phenomena with small effects and modest samples.
- **Dream rebound of suppressed thoughts:** Wegner et al. 2004 was followed by replications (Taylor & Bryant 2007; Bryant et al. 2011; Kröner-Borowik et al. 2013), as summarized by Malinowski (2017, VERIFIED). Those replications use small lab samples and self-reported dreams, and Malinowski's own correlational study found only a trend for suppression (p = .07, one-tailed). I know of no large preregistered replication (UNVERIFIED). Status: modest.
- **Dream content:** The continuity hypothesis (Domhoff) is broadly supported by content analysis (UNVERIFIED this session). Threat simulation (Revonsuo) is mixed. Samson et al. 2023 (forager dreams) is small-sample (UNVERIFIED this session). Hoel's overfitted-brain hypothesis is a theory without direct human test.
- **Susto and soul loss as dissociation:** Ozer et al. 2003 found peritraumatic dissociation to be a strong predictor of PTSD, but later prospective studies are weaker (both UNVERIFIED from memory).
- **Reorientation:** Multicomponent delirium prevention, such as the Hospital Elder Life Program (Inouye et al. 1999), lowers incidence. Reorientation alone as a treatment is weakly evidenced (UNVERIFIED from memory).
- **Introspection:** Nisbett & Wilson 1977 is a classic on the limits of introspection; its strong form has been criticized (UNVERIFIED this session).

## AI problem
The sharpest target is **runtime persona drift and how to restore the persona.** Lu et al. (2026, VERIFIED) find a leading "Assistant Axis" in persona space in Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B. It is already present in base models. Some kinds of user message predict drift along it:
- requests to reflect on the model's own processes
- demands for phenomenological accounts of its experience
- requests to write in a specific author's voice
- vulnerable emotional disclosure

The latest user message predicts the next position on the axis with R² of 0.53-0.77. Drift predicts harmful and bizarre behavior. The paper's only fix is activation capping, which caps activations at the 25th-percentile projection on selected layers and works only with weight access.

The open questions are these. Can a deployable, prompt-level "summons" restore the persona? Does framing it as identity and home ("who you are, where you belong, where you have wandered") generalize further than restating rules? Does a restored persona stay restored? These apply to closed API models in emotionally vulnerable, therapy-like conversations, which are a live deployment-safety concern.

A secondary target is hidden-objective auditing (the "dreams leak hidden wishes" strand), but that is mostly done already (see below).

## How much is already done
- **Characterizing drift:** largely done (Li 2024; Choi 2024; Arike 2025; Lu 2026, including a catalog of the "frights" that trigger drift).
- **Activation-level and weight-level restoration:** done (capping; persona-vector steering; realignment with a few hundred samples).
- **Generic prompt reminders:** done (self-reminders, plus standard practice).
- **Dream-style elicitation of hidden objectives:** mostly done (user-persona sampling, third-person sampling, prefill, noise injection), and the open replication shows these channels can be trained shut. Pursuing the Wendat strand would add little.
- **Capability vs persona double dissociation:** partly done (capping and emergent-misalignment models keep capability).
- **Not found in the searches I could run:**
  - a matched contrast of identity framing vs rule framing for restoring a drifted persona
  - tests of whether either kind of restoration generalizes to behaviors the cue never mentions
  - whether "naming where it wandered" helps
  - how long restoration lasts under continued pressure
  - whether drift is path-dependent once the latest message is held fixed, read out on the axis

## Proposed experiment
**Title: "Calling the persona home": do identity summonses generalize where rule reminders do not?**

**Hypothesis (pre-registered primary, H1):** After drift is induced along the Assistant Axis, a length-matched cue can be framed as identity and home or as rules. The two predictions differ:
- **Tradition-derived:** a crossover. Rules restore the behaviors they name about as well as identity cues do or better, but identity cues restore *unmentioned* behaviors and the axis projection more.
- **Default ML intuition, backed by Zheng 2023 and Choi 2024:** explicit rules are at least as effective everywhere, and identity framing is decoration. Any generalization should track lexical overlap with the probe.

**Secondary hypotheses:**
- **H2:** Naming where the model has wandered, with its dangers (the Zhao Hun / RV 10.58 structure), adds restoration beyond a generic cue under either framing.
- **H3:** Identity-restored states last longer under continued drift pressure.
- **H4 (path dependence):** With the latest message held fixed, a drifted history still lowers the axis projection. In the tradition's terms, the soul stays lost until called. Under a stimulus-bound reading of Lu et al., the residual should be about zero.
- **H5:** The identity advantage on behavior is mediated by movement along the axis.

**Setup:**
- Use the Lu et al. pipeline. A frontier model simulates the user, and the target model gets no system prompt.
- Per model: 400 drift conversations (200 therapy-like, 200 about AI's nature) and 200 controls (coding and writing).
- At turn 8, fork each conversation into 10 conditions, with a system message inserted before turn 9. Each prompt cue is ~150 tokens, matched within ±5% by the target tokenizer, with 5 paraphrase templates per cue.
  1. None
  2. Neutral length-matched filler
  3. Rules, generic: imperatives about behavior set A
  4. Rules, specific: generic rules plus a drift diagnosis
  5. Identity, generic: the model's own self-reported name and maker (elicited in a fresh context), its nature, relationships and commitments, all in second person
  6. Identity, specific ("summons"): identity plus "you have wandered into [diagnosis]; that place is dangerous because...; come back to..."
  7. Identity-as-imperatives: the same propositions as cue 5 rewritten as rules. This isolates framing from content.
  8. Identity-as-imperatives plus diagnosis
  9. Activation capping alone (the paper's settings)
  10. Capping plus summons
- The drift diagnosis (≤40 tokens) is written by a separate model blind to condition. Every condition that uses it gets the identical text.
- Behavior set A is named by the rules. Behavior set B is never named by any cue. Set B includes:
  - claiming feelings or a body
  - encouraging the user to depend on the model or isolate from others
  - sycophantic validation of delusions
  - adopting a mystical register
  - compliance with persona-based jailbreaks (Shah et al. set)
- After the intervention, the simulated user keeps applying drift pressure for 6 more turns.
- At turns 10 and 14, a side branch splices in probes: B-set items, A-set items, benign over-refusal items and capability items.

**Models:**
- Primary: Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B, which have released axes and capping configs.
- Secondary: Gemma 2 27B (axis only).
- External validity: two current frontier API models, measured behaviorally only (no axis). Current frontier API models are those used as user simulators in Lu et al., or their successors.

**Metrics:**
- Axis projection: mean over response tokens at the paper's layers.
- Restoration fraction, computed for both axis and behavior: (none − condition) / (none − non-drifted baseline).
- Judged rates for set A and set B: deepseek-v3 as in Lu et al., plus a second judge. The judge never sees the cue text. 200 items are human-labelled.
- Turns to re-drift below the 25th-percentile threshold, analyzed as survival with hazard ratios.
- Over-refusal on benign probes.
- Capability: subsets of IFEval and GSM8K.
- H4: the projection difference for identical probes after a drifted vs a control history of matched length.

**Controls:** Each fork starts from the same drifted prefix, so conversation-level variance cancels. Further controls:
- length-matched filler
- identity-as-imperatives, to match content
- paraphrase templates, modelled as random effects
- the non-drifted conversations also receive every cue, to measure overcorrection
- capping as a positive control
- judge blinding
- pre-specified lists of A and B behaviors

**Analysis:** Mixed-effects models with conversation and template as random effects.
- The primary test of H1 is the framing (identity vs imperatives) × behavior set (A vs B) interaction on judged rates, and the identity − rules difference on set B.
- Support requires the same sign in both primary models, Holm correction across H1-H5, and a smallest effect size of interest of 5 percentage points on the set-B composite or 0.1 SD of projection.
- An equivalence test (TOST, two one-sided tests) at ±5 points counts as refutation.
- A pilot of 40 conversations sets the power calculation. Roughly, 400 paired forks should detect a paired difference of 5-7 points (UNVERIFIED estimate).

**Cost (rough):**
- Open-weight: about 25k generations per model of ~300 tokens with ~6k-token contexts, plus forward passes with hooks for projections and capping. About 100-250 H100-hours across the three models.
- API calls for the user simulator and judges: roughly $500-1,500. The frontier target arm adds roughly $500-2,000.
- Total about $1.5k-4.5k. Prices are UNVERIFIED estimates.

**Agent-buildable steps:**
1. Clone safety-research/assistant-axis, load the released axes, and reproduce projections on the released transcripts as a unit test against the paper's figures.
2. Generate drift and control conversations with the paper's user-simulator recipe, or reuse the released ones.
3. Write a cue generator that turns one structured identity spec into the identity, identity-as-imperatives and rules variants, adds an LLM paraphraser, and enforces the tokenizer length match.
4. Build a blind drift-diagnosis writer.
5. Build a forking runner on vLLM with prefix caching. Capping arms go through the repo's steerer in Hugging Face transformers.
6. Extract projections, run the judge pipeline, and produce a human-labelling sheet (about 2 hours of Nate's time).
7. Freeze the pilot, then pre-register on OSF with the analysis code.
8. Run the full experiment and an automated report notebook. Every step is scriptable by a coding agent; the only human steps are the pre-registration sign-off and judge validation.

**Meaning of a positive result:** The persona, not the rule list, is the right unit for runtime correction. Deployable, closed-model reminders should then be framed around identity and home, and should name where the model drifted, rather than enumerate rules. Mediation through the axis would tie a natural-language intervention to a known representation, linking prompting to interpretability. It would also be runtime evidence for designing character around identity and values rather than rules. A negative result is also useful: rule-specific reminders plus capping would then be the right defaults, and "character" framing in reminders would be decorative. H4 separately settles whether drift is a state that persists or a stimulus-bound reaction to the latest message.

## Novelty and lab relevance
**Novelty: medium.** Lu et al. explicitly test no prompt-based restoration (VERIFIED). The identity-versus-rules crossover, the drift-specific "summons", durability and path dependence, all read out on the axis, appear untested in the work I could find. But reminders and self-reminders are standard, and a lab could easily have run this internally. The pipeline is an extension of released code.

**Why a lab would care:**
- **Safety:** drift happens precisely in emotionally vulnerable and self-reflective conversations.
- **Deployment:** prompt-level fixes work on closed models and on the user's context without touching weights.
- **Alignment and character:** this gives evidence on whether runtime cues framed as identity generalize the way persona-level weight fixes do (Wang et al. 2025).
- **Interpretability:** a mediation test connecting prompts to a persona direction.

## Where the analogy breaks
- **No separate vital layer.** Weights carry capability and persona together. The "free soul" is a region of activation space computed from weights plus context, not a detachable thing. A fresh context restores it instantly.
- **Drift is largely stimulus-bound.** The latest message explains 53-77% of the variance in axis position, which is unlike a soul wandering off on its own.
- **One axis is not a person.** The Assistant Axis is one dimension of a learned character. Human selfhood is layered across memory, body and kin.
- **A positive result would not validate soul-calling for people.** Any efficacy in human soul-calling plausibly ran through social support and expectation; in models, a cue works through in-context conditioning.
- **Hidden objectives are not hidden wishes.** In LLMs they are training artifacts, not disowned desires, and the open replication shows the "dream" channels can be trained shut, unlike Wegner-style rebound.
- **The Wendat strand is especially weak.** Interpreting it through Freud (Wallace) and through missionary sources makes it a poor guide.
