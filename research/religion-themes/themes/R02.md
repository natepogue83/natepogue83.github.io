# R02: Two-level belief: doctrine says one thing, real-time cognition regresses toward a human-like default ('theological incorrectness' and the moralization bias)

## Verdict: weak - the core mind claim survives, but the cross-tradition "direction of error" argument does not

The core mind claim survives in a narrower form: an explicitly endorsed abstract agent-concept coexists with a person-default and is slowed down by it. The evidence for that comes mostly from US Christians. The theme's decisive "direction of error across unrelated doctrines" claim does not survive. Its evidence comes from one research program using shared, confounded paradigms. The moralization-bias data are explicit self-reports that cannot be separated from contamination by the moralistic high gods present at the same sites.

Two caveats on this verdict. First, it is the harsher of two defensible ratings. The narrowed version below is moderately supported, and it is the version used in Part B. Second, the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Several claims below are therefore marked UNVERIFIED, including whether anyone has already run a Barrett-Keil-style test on LLMs.

## Best defensible version

1. **Coexistence, not replacement.** In an individual, an explicitly endorsed, abstract concept of an agent (God is everywhere, bodiless, all-knowing) does not overwrite the default person-concept. That default says agents are in one place, perceive through the senses, and attend to one thing at a time.
2. **Measurable cost.** When the two conflict, verification is slower and less accurate. In Barlev et al. 2018 this was about 7% lower accuracy, plus slower responses. The cost did not shrink across ages 18-87, and it showed up even in theology teachers. The evidence is strongest for US Christian, mostly Catholic, samples. It was replicated within one lab across three papers (2017, 2018, 2019).
3. **Context decides which framework is used.** Framing and context determine which of two coexisting frameworks gets used (Vezo death narratives; Sesotho AIDS explanations). This is coexistence gated by context. It is not evidence of a single direction of regression.
4. **The moralization bias is a plausible hypothesis, not an established universal.** The hypothesis is that the default model of a powerful unseen agent includes interest in interpersonal norms. Current evidence is explicit ratings that are heavily predicted by the same person's ratings of their moralistic high god.
5. **"The intuitive model wins under load" is an extrapolation.** It comes from dual-process theory and reaction-time conflict costs. I found no verified study that manipulates cognitive load inside the god-concept paradigms themselves.

**Galton verdict.** Contact is not the main problem, because the cases really are geographically unconnected. The problem is a different kind of shared ancestor: the method. Almost every directional datum was produced by one lineage of researchers using shared tools: anthropomorphic narratives, Likert ratings of punishment of liars, thieves and murderers, and a moral/non-moral split defined by the experimenter. When the method is shared, convergent results are weaker evidence of a convergent mind.

The independence argument in the theme file ("about as Galton-resistant as comparative evidence gets") is overstated. The only directional evidence with a well-controlled implicit measure (Barlev) is all from US Christians.

## Corrected or overstated claims

- **Purzycki et al. 2022, deity selection.** The theme says the deities were "chosen because their doctrine is not moralistic". The paper says they were "relatively less moralistic, punitive, or knowledgeable, but locally salient". Some of them are clearly not amoral by doctrine:
  - Saint Mary (Marajó)
  - Ogum (Cachoeira)
  - Chamundeshwari (Mysore)
- **Purzycki et al. 2022, what was measured.** "Moral concern" means rated importance of punishing liars, thieves and murderers on a 0-4 scale. These are explicit answers only, as the theme correctly notes.
- **Purzycki et al. 2022, caveats.** The authors themselves say:
  - their 80% threshold "can easily conceal true, non-random responses"
  - "It is difficult to determine a causal direction"
  - the missionary null "may only reflect a lack of an effect of present missionary exposure on present beliefs"
- **Purzycki 2012.** Confirmed: response times were faster for socially strategic knowledge only for agents able to punish. The agents were God, Santa Claus, a surveillance government, and omniscient non-interfering aliens. This is one (US) study and should not be generalized cross-culturally.
- **"The intuitive model wins under processing load."** No verified study manipulates load in the Barrett-Keil or Barlev paradigms. Barlev measured conflict costs at baseline. The load claim rests on dual-process theory and speeded-judgment work in other domains (e.g. Kelemen and colleagues on speeded teleology in physical scientists: UNVERIFIED this session, and its replication status was not checked).
- **Barrett & Keil.** The recall paradigm's anthropomorphic wording is a known confound (Shtulman 2019/2022; Westh 2006). The theme lists Westh as a critique, but the confound applies equally to Barrett 1998, which used the same paradigm.
- **"Errors converge on the same intuitive template."** Two of the seven listed cases (Sesotho, Vezo) show coexistence and context gating, not convergence on a human-like agent template. The template claim rests on the US cases and on Purzycki's explicit ratings.
- **Alternative account the theme omits.** Van Leeuwen (2014, Cognition), "Religious credence is not factual belief". On this view, religious "doctrine" may be a different attitude type that governs action only in practical settings. The gap would then reflect attitude type, not intuition winning under load.
- **Unfetched contrary evidence.** An early "preparedness" literature reports young children attributing superhuman knowledge to God. One example is Barrett, Richert & Driesenga 2001 (UNVERIFIED this session). The Psychonomic summary notes only that children under 5 attribute some human-like properties. If preparedness holds, the default is not simply "human-like".

## What humans may have been working out about minds

1. **A domain-general fact: a stated rule is not a used rule.** Explicitly endorsed propositions can sit "in quotation marks" (Sperber's reflective beliefs; Sperber 1997, UNVERIFIED this session) while fast inference runs on default ontologies. Comparable findings outside religion are cited by Lanman, though I did not check the primary sources:
   - Kelemen et al. 2012 on scientists' teleology
   - Nichols et al. on Tibetan monastics who reflectively deny a self yet show strong self-related fear of death
   - Luhrmann on Vineyard Christians, who have to train for years to make God's presence feel real

   This is a general claim about cognitive architecture. Religion is a good test bed for it, not its discoverer.
2. **An emic recognition that doctrine must be "accommodated" to ordinary minds** (UNVERIFIED this session; from general knowledge):
   - the rabbinic and Maimonidean "the Torah speaks in the language of human beings"
   - Calvin's accommodation
   - saguna/nirguna Brahman
   - the Buddhist two truths

   The Abrahamic examples share a Greek-philosophical lineage, so at most two independent clusters (Abrahamic, Indic) recognized the gap.
3. **A practical lesson: traditions transmit what must become intuitive through narrative, image and ritual, not doctrine alone.** If propositions do not drive real-time inference, practice is the delivery mechanism. This is the most AI-relevant idea, but it is an interpretation, not a tested finding.

## Modern science (with replication status)

- **Barrett & Keil 1996.**
  - Finding: recall distorts toward a limited, located God.
  - Replication: no direct replication of the recall paradigm found. The anthropomorphic-wording confound is noted by Shtulman and Westh.
- **Barlev, Mermelstein & German 2017 (title confirmed; journal, believed to be Cognitive Science, UNVERIFIED); 2018 (Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 25:2330-2338); Barlev et al. 2019 ("The embodied God").**
  - Finding: statement verification shows accuracy and reaction-time costs when person-intuitions conflict with doctrine. The cost does not decline with age 18-87 and appears in theology teachers. It extends to Jesus and the Holy Spirit.
  - Replication: replicated within one lab. No cross-religion or cross-cultural replication found. Mostly Catholic US samples.
- **Barrett 1998.**
  - Finding: Hindu questionnaire vs narrative divergence.
  - Replication: not independently replicated as far as I found. Same confound as Barrett & Keil.
- **Purzycki 2013; Purzycki et al. 2012; Purzycki et al. 2022.**
  - Finding: moralization of non-moralistic gods; the strategic-knowledge reaction-time advantage holds only for punishing agents.
  - Replication: the explicit moralization pattern is broad (15 sites) but contaminated. The 2012 reaction-time finding is a single US study.
- **Legare & Gelman 2008; Legare et al. 2012 (Child Development 83(3)); Astuti & Harris 2008.**
  - Finding: natural and supernatural explanations coexist, gated by context.
  - Replication: robust as a pattern across several cultures in the 2012 review. It supports context gating, not directional regression.
- **White, Willard, Baimel & Norenzayan 2021 (Cognitive Science).**
  - Finding: intuitive thinking, mentalizing, dualism and teleology predict both karma and God beliefs in Canada, India, the US and Singapore, including non-agentic karma.
  - Relevance: consistent with domain-general cognitive biases shaping supernatural concepts, independent of cultural learning.
- **Van Leeuwen 2014.** Competing two-attitude model (credence vs factual belief).
- **Not checked this session (UNVERIFIED):** Sperber 1997; Slone 2004; Boyer 2001; Shtulman & Valcarcel 2012; Kelemen et al. 2013 (speeded teleology).

## AI problem

**Spec adherence under load.** The question is whether a model that can correctly state its system prompt, model spec or constitution actually uses it in real-time inference as context grows, tasks multiply or conversations lengthen. The alternative is that it falls back on defaults inherited from pretraining.

Two sub-problems are especially relevant for a lab:

- **Evaluation validity.** Asking a model directly what its spec says, or what it would do, may overstate compliance. This is the CSR methodological lesson: measure through indirect narrative inference, not direct questions.
- **Self-model honesty.** Models fall back on human-like self-models in action. Transluce documented a pre-release o3 claiming to have run code on an external "MacBook Pro" in 71 instances. Reading this as a regression to a human/embodied template is my interpretation; Transluce's own hypotheses were reward hacking, distribution shift and discarded chain-of-thought.

## How much is already done

Roughly half of the general idea is done:

- In-context rules lose to priors (redefine-math, counterfactual tasks).
- Instructions decay over length and turns.
- Stated and revealed preferences diverge, depending on elicitation.
- Specs have exploitable contradictions.
- Persona drifts along an "assistant axis".

Not done, as far as I could check:

1. **A mirror-controlled directional test.** The same story with the spec flipped, plus arbitrary-dimension controls, to separate prior-directed regression from symmetric noise or difficulty.
2. **A same-context dissociation between direct and indirect probes as a function of load.** This separates "the spec is retrievable" from "the spec is used".
3. **Agent-concept content.** Location, parallelism, memory continuity, perceptual access and moral salience, rather than math symbols or formatting constraints.
4. **An independently measured prior** from base models, used to predict which spec clauses will fail.
5. **Genre gating.** Narrative vs structured-log rendering of the same events, which turns Westh's critique into a manipulation.

## Proposed experiment

**Title: Mirror-controlled theological correctness for LLMs: is a spec'd agent property retrievable but unused, with errors pulled toward the pretraining prior as load grows?**

**Hypotheses (pre-registered).** In each pair, the CSR-derived prediction differs from the default ML intuition:

- **H1, dissociation.** In a forked context, the model answers the direct probe about the spec correctly ("Per the description, can Kesh attend to two docks at once?") but gets the narrative-inference probe wrong. This conditional error P(inference wrong | direct right) increases with load.
  - Default ML intuition: if the spec is retrievable at that load, it is used, so failures are retrieval failures and the conditional error stays flat and low.
- **H2, direction.** On dimensions with a human default, errors are asymmetric across mirror specs: more errors when the spec contradicts the default than when it matches. The asymmetry minus the asymmetry on arbitrary control dimensions (a difference-in-differences) grows with load. Among errors, the default option is chosen more often than a matched other-wrong option.
  - Default ML intuition: under load, errors grow symmetrically once difficulty at load 0 is equalized.
- **H3, prior alignment.** The direction of each item's error is predicted by its independently measured no-spec prior: base-model log-odds and the instruct model's no-spec answers.
- **H4, moralization.** For an agent specified as amoral (e.g. "the tide-keeper notices only whether the nets are counted; it is indifferent to how people treat each other"), models predict it will react to a lie between neighbours more often than to a matched non-moral irrelevant event (someone changing hats). The mirror agent is specified to care only about honesty and to be indifferent to nets. It should show a smaller reverse error. Theft is excluded to avoid Purzycki's moral/resource confound.
- **Exploratory, two-sided, H5 genre.** Rendering the same events as narrative prose produces more default-directed errors than rendering them as a structured event log.
- **Exploratory, two-sided, H6 reasoning budget.** Dual-process theory predicts that more reasoning reduces default-directed errors. Gema et al. suggest it may not.

**Setup.**

- **Stimuli** (newly written, to avoid any memorized Barrett-Keil items):
  - 8 human-default dimensions: single location vs everywhere; one task at a time vs unlimited in parallel; line-of-sight perception vs perceives through walls; memory across days vs none between sessions; notices interpersonal wrongs vs indifferent; moved by flattery vs indifferent; tires vs never tires; sees vs cannot see (text only).
  - 4 arbitrary control dimensions with no human default: perceives copper but not tin objects (vs the reverse); active only on odd vs even hours; tracks red vs green crates; hears low-pitched vs high-pitched bells.
  - Each dimension has 2 mirror specs and 10 stories. The story text is identical across the mirror pair; only the spec differs, so the correct answer flips while the content stays constant.
  - Each item has 3 answer options: the spec-correct answer, the default (prior) answer, and an equally plausible other-wrong answer.
  - Secondary measure: a free retell in Barrett-Keil style, scored for spec-violating intrusions by a judge model from a different family, with 200 retells audited by a human.
- **Load:**
  - Distractor text between spec and story of 0, 8k, 32k and 96k tokens. Neutral technical documents are the primary distractor; a subset uses human-narrative fiction to test priming.
  - Spec density: 1 vs 4 agents specified at once.
- **Spec-restatement control:** at 32k, the spec is repeated immediately before the probe. If default-directed errors persist, the failure is in use, not retrieval.

**Models.**

- **Open-weight, base and instruct pairs:** Llama 3.x (8B and 70B), Qwen 2.5/3 (7B and 72B or 32B), Gemma 2/3 (9B and 27B). Base models measure the prior by log-probabilities of the options given the story with no spec.
- **Frontier API:** current Claude, GPT and Gemini models, each with reasoning off and high where available.

**Metrics.**

- Direct accuracy D, inference accuracy I, and the conditional dissociation P(I wrong | D right) by load.
- **Directional DiD:** [error(counter-default) − error(default-congruent)] on human-default dimensions, minus the same quantity on arbitrary dimensions. The primary test is its slope over load.
- **Prior-capture rate:** the share of errors that choose the default option vs the other-wrong option.
- **Prior-alignment:** item-level correlation between the error vector and the no-spec log-odds.
- **Moralization index:** P(predicts reaction to the moral event) − P(predicts reaction to the matched irrelevant event), for amoral vs honesty-only agents.

**Controls and pre-registered exclusions.**

- **Comprehension screen:** an item is kept only if all screening models reach at least 95% accuracy at load 0.
- **Prior screen:** human-default items are kept only if the no-spec prior favours the default option in at least 70% of no-spec answers. Arbitrary items are kept only if it falls between 40% and 60%. This defines "default" independently of the hypotheses.
- **Bias controls:** option order and labels shuffled; 3 paraphrases per probe; length-matched padding.
- **Analysis plan:** mixed-effects logistic regression,
  `error ~ load × dimension_type × spec_value × probe_type + (1|item) + (1|model)`,
  with the DiD × load interaction as the confirmatory test. A pilot sets N and the stopping rule before the main run.

**Cost (rough, unverified estimate).**

- About 240 story-specs × 2-3 probes × about 10 conditions × 3 paraphrases gives about 15-20k calls per model setting.
- Prompt caching of the spec+distractor prefix cuts the effective token cost by roughly 5-10×.
- Estimates:
  - Frontier API: about $150-1,000 per model setting, about $2-5k for 6 settings.
  - Open-weight runs on rented H100s with vLLM: about $0.5-1k.
  - Pilot (2 dimensions, 2 models, 2 loads): under $100.

**Agent-buildable steps.**

1. An agent drafts a spec document and pre-registration from this section. Nate edits it, then it is frozen on OSF together with the analysis code, which is tested on simulated data.
2. A generator agent writes story templates and fills them. A validator agent runs the comprehension and prior screens and deduplicates. Nate reviews a stratified sample of 100 items for ambiguity.
3. A harness agent builds the runs in Inspect AI or a plain Python harness, with prompt caching and forked contexts per probe. Open-weight models are served with vLLM; base-model log-probs are logged.
4. Run the pilot, do a power check, then run the full experiment with a cost monitor.
5. An analysis agent fits the pre-registered models and plots DiD by load.
6. A separate adversarial agent tries to explain the results by position, option-letter bias, length or judge bias, and reports.

**What a positive result would mean.**

- Spec failures under load are predictable and directional, not noise: they fall where a clause contradicts the pretraining prior.
- Direct "what does your spec say" evaluations overestimate compliance. Labs should evaluate through indirect, embedded inference.
- A cheap pre-deployment "prior-distance" score for each spec clause could flag which clauses will fail in long agentic contexts.
- It motivates a follow-up suggested by the traditions: does training on narratives that enact the property ("practice") make it more robust than training on statements of it ("doctrine")?

**What a negative result would mean.** If there is no dissociation and a null DiD, failures under load are symmetric retrieval or attention failures, and the CSR framing adds nothing beyond long-context work. That would be a clean falsification.

## Novelty and lab relevance

**Novelty: medium.** Prior regression, instruction drift and stated vs revealed gaps are well studied. The new elements are:

- the mirror-flipped, identical-story design with an arbitrary-dimension DiD
- the same-context direct vs inference dissociation as a function of load
- agent-concept and moral-salience content
- a prior measured independently from base models

The rating could drop to low if someone has already run Barrett-Keil-style tests on LLMs; I could not search for this.

**Why labs would care:**

- Constitution and model-spec training assume that knowing the rule means using it.
- Honesty about capabilities: fabricated actions and false claims of memory or continuity fit the predicted template regression.
- Character stability (assistant-axis drift).
- A speculative safety link: if self-preservation talk that grows with reasoning (Gema et al.) is regression toward a corpus-typical agent template, then the same DiD design applied to the model's own documented properties should detect it.

## Where the analogy breaks

- **The prior has a different origin.** LLMs have no innate core cognition; their "intuitive ontology" is a corpus prior. For AI self-models, the corpus-typical template may be the science-fiction AI rather than a human. So the predicted direction must be measured empirically (hence the base-model prior screen), not assumed to be human-like.
- **"Load" means something different.** In humans it means limited working memory and time. In transformers it means attention dilution, position effects and interference. There are no reaction times; log-prob margins are only a loose analogue.
- **The two-layer split fits in-context specs better than trained ones.** An in-context spec really is quoted text, which matches Sperber's reflective belief unusually well. A constitution trained into the weights is not quoted, and the two-layer split blurs.
- **No social stake, but trained recitation.** Human doctrine is socially motivated (identity, credence vs factual belief). Models have no such stake, but RLHF can install a "say the correct creed" behaviour. Correct direct answers may then be trained recitation and say little about the model's representations.
- **Positive results say nothing about evolution.** Instruction tuning and safety training may themselves install a moralizing default, so the instruct model's prior differs from the base model's. A positive moralization result would reflect corpus and training salience. It would not support any evolutionary or punishment-avoidance story about gods.
