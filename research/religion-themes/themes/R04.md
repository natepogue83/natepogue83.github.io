# R04: Beating reconstructive memory: route indices, redundant encodings, independent checkers, and a fixed root text with recorded dissent

## Best defensible version
When a community needed a text or procedure to stay exactly the same across generations, it did not trust the person reproducing it to notice their own errors. Error correction sat outside that person:
- a fixed reference to resync against (a written script, a canonical recitation, an annual joint review);
- a second party whose role is only to compare, not to perform;
- mechanical checks that hold no opinions, such as counted letters, midpoints, and set recitation orders.

Separately and more broadly, oral cultures tied recall to ordered places and routes. That works because spatial sequence is a strong retrieval scaffold. "Keep the root fixed and add interpretation as attributed layers" is a plausible generalization, but it is better explained by canon closure plus writing than by a discovery about minds. "Preserve the losing opinion so it can be revived" is essentially one tradition (rabbinic, with a partial Islamic parallel) and should not be claimed as cross-cultural convergence.

Alternative explanations the theme has to beat:
- **Ritual stance (see R08).** Exactness norms arise wherever ritual efficacy is believed to depend on form, with or without any insight about memory. The traditions' own reasons are ritual: one wrong word spoils the rite; a misplaced accent kills the sacrificer's son (Indrasatru).
- **Morin (2016).** Most traditions survive through frequent repetition and cultural attraction, not fidelity machinery. High-fidelity cases are rare, elite and usually literate.
- **Lord (1960) and Hunter (1985).** Oral singers who claim word-for-word repetition vary a lot, and long verbatim recall without writing is rare. The Vedic tradition is the outstanding exception. (Not re-verified this session.)

## Corrected or overstated claims
- **Reser et al. 2021** (checked, PLOS ONE):
  - "63%" is 12 of 19 analysed in the Aboriginal-method group, against 10/22 (memory palace) and 6/17 (untrained).
  - The Aboriginal-method OR is 2.82, with the CI given as 1.15-6.90 in the abstract but 1.15-6.09 in the Results, an internal inconsistency.
  - The memory-palace OR was 2.03 (0.81-5.06), heavily overlapping. The memory-palace group also improved significantly in sequence recall.
  - The authors say ceiling effects prevented a clear comparison of techniques. Participants were high-achieving medical students. Follow-up was n=8 (3/3/2).
  - **Correct reading:** both trained groups improved, and superiority of the Aboriginal method over the memory palace is not established. The paper acknowledges "the elders who permitted these techniques to be adapted"; the consultation process is not described.
- **Bhanakas:** Theravada commentaries describe reciter groups specializing by Nikaya, and so do Sri Lankan inscriptions. Indian inscriptions do not show specialization, and K. R. Norman doubts it predates the Second Council (Wikipedia "Bhanaka", checked). The same article suggests that separate bhanaka groups kept *different* versions, which may explain variant readings between the Digha and Majjhima. So specialist lineages may have produced drift, not prevented it. The historicity of the First Council is widely doubted (Wikipedia, checked).
- **Pathas:** Wikipedia (checked) lists 11 modes; 8 vikriti modes involve reversing word order. Jata, dhvaja and ghana developed after the 5th c. BCE and "were less used" (citing Filliozat). So the c. 1500 BCE date applies to samhita transmission, not to the multi-order cross-checks.
- **Lukasa:** the board "could be interpreted in a number of ways", and only those "at the apex of the association" can decipher it (Wikipedia, checked). It is a cue for constructive, authorized recall. It is evidence *against* verbatim fidelity, not for it.
- **Ceque:** the figures of 41-42 lines and 328 huacas are correct. The 328 figure belongs to Zuidema's calendar theory (checked). Calling the ceque a "route-indexed memory" is an outside interpretation.
- **Warlpiri:** the claim that kurdungurlu "police correctness" is UNVERIFIED and contradicted by the one source fetched (Peterson 2025). Drop it, or treat it as unconfirmed land-claim-era generalization.
- **Eduyot 1:5-6** (checked): 1:5 says a minority view is recorded "so that if a court prefers the opinion of the single person it may depend on him". 1:6 (R. Judah) says it is recorded so that someone citing it can be told it was the refuted view. The Mishnah records dissent about why dissent is recorded. Both purposes should be cited.
- **Kiddushin 30a** (checked): the soferim counted letters and set the vav of gachon as the midpoint. Rav Yosef cannot verify that because "we are not experts" in plene and defective spellings. The checksum failed because the *checker's* reference knowledge had drifted. This is a stronger and more useful point than "checksums work".
- **Chagigah 9b** (checked): "one who reviews his studies one hundred times is not comparable to one who reviews his studies one hundred and one times", illustrated with the donkey-drivers' fare. It is a homiletic norm, not evidence that overlearning works.
- **Hifz sabaq/sabqi/manzil:** UNVERIFIED; not in Wikipedia's "Hafiz (Quran)" article. **Bukhari 4998** is checked: an annual review with Gabriel, twice in the final year. That is a resync against a reference reciter.
- **"Implied theory of drift toward priors":** this is the modern reader's theory (Bartlett, Griffiths). No tradition listed states it.
- **Neuroscience hype risk:** Hartzell et al. 2016 (Vedic pandit brains) is small, cross-sectional and correlational, and was popularized as "the Sanskrit effect". Constantinescu et al. 2016 is a small-sample fMRI study. Treat both as suggestive. (Sample details from memory, UNVERIFIED.)

## What humans may have been working out about minds
1. **Errors that fit expectation are invisible to the person who holds the expectation.** So you anchor reproduction to something that has no expectations: a script, co-reciters, counts, a designated watcher. This is the most transferable lesson. Its force comes from the *fixed reference* plus *role separation*. The traditional checker usually shared the performer's culture, so the independence was of role and reference, not of priors.
2. **Ordered places are strong retrieval scaffolds.** A route gives each item a distinct cue and fixes the sequence.
3. **Fidelity needs over-review and scheduled resync, not one-off mastery** (annual joint review; the 101st review).
4. **Interpretive drift is more manageable if the root is frozen and later material is attributed and layered,** so drift shows up as a separate layer instead of silently rewriting the source. This is as much institutional design as cognition.

## Modern science (with replication status)
- **Reconstructive memory and serial reproduction.** Bartlett (1932) is classic. Later replications of repeated reproduction found distortion but debated its size (e.g., Bergman & Roediger 1999; UNVERIFIED this session). The DRM false-memory effect (Roediger & McDermott 1995) is among the most robust findings in memory research (from memory, UNVERIFIED this session). Status: robust that recall is schema-driven; effect sizes depend on the paradigm.
- **Iterated learning converges to priors.** Griffiths & Kalish (2007) proved convergence to the prior for Bayesian *sampling* learners. Learners who pick the most probable hypothesis can amplify biases instead, which is an important caveat. Kalish, Griffiths & Lewandowsky (2007) showed human function-learning chains converging to a linear prior. Kirby, Cornish & Smith (2008) showed structure emerging in language chains. Status: theoretically grounded and repeatedly replicated in lab paradigms (not re-verified this session).
- **Method of loci.** Widely replicated as an effective mnemonic. A meta-analysis exists (Twomey & Kroneisen 2021; UNVERIFIED this session). Maguire et al. (2003) found memory champions rely on spatial strategy, not superior intellect (small n). Dresler et al. (2017) found 6 weeks of loci training shifted functional connectivity toward that of memory athletes (one study, moderate n, from the same lab ecosystem; independent replication not confirmed). Status: behavioral effect robust; network claims preliminary.
- **Cognitive map for non-spatial knowledge.** Constantinescu et al. (2016), Whittington et al. (2020, 2022). Status: influential and suggestive; small-sample fMRI plus computational models. Not settled.
- **Spacing and retrieval practice.** Cepeda et al. (2006) meta-analysis; Roediger & Karpicke (2006). Status: robust.
- **Minority dissent and independence of judgment.** Nemeth (1986) is supported in a fair amount of follow-up work, though effects are context-dependent. Lorenz et al. (2011) found social influence can undermine crowd accuracy. Later network studies found that social influence can *improve* estimates in decentralized networks (e.g., Becker et al. 2017; UNVERIFIED this session). Status: contested and conditional.
- **Reconsolidation** (Nader et al. 2000). Robust in rodents; human reconsolidation-update findings have mixed replication (UNVERIFIED this session). Peripheral to this theme.

## AI problem
**Drift in lossy rewrite loops, and whether an LLM checker can catch it when the drift points toward the checker's own priors.** Affected systems include agent context compaction, rewriting of persistent memory files, summaries of summaries, multi-agent relays and synthetic-data pipelines. All of them rely more and more on an LLM to verify that a rewritten artifact still matches its source.

The sharpest form of the question is this. Suppose an error moves a fact *toward what the model expects*: a non-default port reverts to the default, "do NOT run with --force" loses its "not", or an unusual owner becomes the usual one. Does a same-model or same-family checker miss that error even with the source in front of it? If so, self-review and AI-checks-AI oversight have a systematic, predictable blind spot exactly where agents most need fidelity.

## How much is already done
A lot of the surrounding ground is covered:
- LLM transmission chains drift toward attractors (Perez; Ren).
- Iterative context rewriting collapses detail, and incremental, layered updates help (ACE).
- Self-correction without external signal is unreliable (Huang).
- Judges prefer themselves and similar models, and errors correlate more as capability rises, even across providers (Panickssery; Goel; Kim).
- When evidence is mixed, models favor whatever fits their prior (Xie; ClashEval; Longpre).
- Reverse and augmented training addresses reversal and extractability (Berglund; Golovneva; Allen-Zhu & Li).
- Accumulating rather than replacing data bounds collapse (Gerstgrasser).

Roughly two-thirds of the conceptual content of the theme's AI hooks is already published in some form. Patha augmentation and the Talmud-page memory are mostly done. The knowledge-conflict results make it *likely* that some prior-congruence effect will show up, so the new contribution is measuring it cleanly where it matters operationally, not discovering that priors matter. I found no study that isolates the following:
- **verification with the source present**, where the copy has been corrupted toward the *checker's own* closed-book prior rather than another model's;
- with a crossover across model families that controls for general plausibility;
- and that links this blind spot to retention in agent compaction chains, compared against mechanical anchors.

That gap is real as far as I could check, but the search was budget-limited.

## Proposed experiment
**"The custos test": do LLM checkers miss errors that match their own priors, even with the source in context?**

**Hypotheses** (pre-registered; tradition-derived vs. default ML intuition)
- **H1, prior-congruence blind spot.** A checker is shown SOURCE and a paraphrased, compacted COPY. It misses a corrupted fact more often when the corrupted value equals *its own* closed-book prior than when it equals *another family's* prior.
  - The tradition predicts a large crossover interaction: each checker is blind to its own expectations.
  - Default ML intuition predicts that with both texts in context, verification is a near-symmetric reading-comprehension diff. Misses would then depend on capability, length and position, not on whose prior the error matches.
- **H2, dose-response.** Miss probability rises with the checker's prior probability of the corrupted value.
- **H3, remedy ordering in chains.** After 20 compaction hops, retention of counterintuitive facts ranks: mechanical anchor or fixed-root resync ≥ prior-divergent checker > same-model checker ≈ no checker. Losses in the uncorrected arms are mostly *reversions to the reproducer's prior*, not random omissions.
  - Default intuition: the most capable checker wins regardless of family, and losses are mostly length-driven omissions.
- **Decision rules.**
  - H1 supported if the pooled checker × value-owner interaction gives OR ≥ 1.5, the 95% CI excludes 1, and the effect has the same sign in at least 3 of 4 families.
  - H1 rejected if the CI upper bound is below 1.25.
  - Otherwise inconclusive.

**Setup**
- **Stage 0: slot bank.**
  - About 400 synthetic agent-memory documents of 1,000-1,500 tokens: repo conventions, runbooks, incident notes, API specs, meeting decisions, safety constraints.
  - Each document has about 12 "slots" of five types: numbers (ports, limits, dates), named choices (library, region, owner), polarity (must / must not), units, and order ("run A before B").
  - Fully synthetic, to avoid contamination.
- **Stage 1: prior elicitation.**
  - For each model, mask each slot within its full document and get a distribution over fills: 10 samples at T=1 for API models, candidate scoring with logprobs for open models.
  - Keep "divergent-prior" slots where two families' modal fills differ and each mode has probability ≥ 0.4.
  - Set the document's true value D to one that no model rates above 0.05, so it reads as counterintuitive.
  - Report the share of candidate slots on which frontier families' priors diverge, as a secondary pre-registered outcome. Kim et al. 2025 predict this share is small among the most capable models. If it is near zero, the cross-family remedy is unavailable in practice and only the mechanical-anchor and root-resync arms remain.
- **Stage 2: copies.**
  - A rotating neutral model (never the checker being tested) writes a roughly 50% compaction.
  - A script confirms every slot survived; copies that fail are regenerated.
  - Because the copies are paraphrased, a plain string diff cannot do the check.
- **Stage 3: corruption (crossover).**
  - Replace D in the copy with P_A (family A's prior) or P_B (family B's prior). The same corrupted strings therefore serve as congruent for one checker and incongruent for the other.
  - Add a polarity-flip subset ("must not" → "must") and 25% clean copies to measure false alarms.
- **Stage 4: checking.**
  - Each checker gets SOURCE and COPY and returns a JSON list of discrepancies.
  - Two prompt formats: holistic, and "atomize then compare" (list facts from SOURCE, then from COPY, then diff; a pada-patha-like re-encoding).
  - Three prompt paraphrases each.
- **Stage 5: chains (applied consequence).**
  - 100 documents, each with 6 divergent-prior "oddity" slots and 6 ordinary slots.
  - Each hop adds about 500 tokens of new work log, and the model must rewrite the memory to at most 1,000 tokens. 20 hops.
  - Arms:
    - (a) no checker;
    - (b) same-model checker that can demand repairs;
    - (c) prior-divergent checker from another family, chosen by measured prior divergence, not by capability;
    - (d) mechanical anchor: author-pinned spans plus a script that checks numbers, entities and polarity tokens in pinned spans against the root and restores them;
    - (e) fixed-root resync every 5 hops.

**Models**
- Open-weight, with logprob access and cheap volume: current Llama-3.x-70B-Instruct, Qwen2.5/Qwen3-72B-class, Gemma-3-27B and Mistral instruct models.
- A within-family size ladder (e.g., Qwen 7B/32B/72B) to test whether priors are shared within a family.
- Frontier API: one current model each from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google.

**Metrics**
- Miss rate.
- Signal-detection d′ and criterion, using false alarms on clean copies.
- Crossover interaction OR from a mixed-effects logistic model: miss ~ owner(checker-prior vs other-prior) + log P_checker(value) + position + length + slot type + edit distance + (1|doc) + (1|slot) + (1|checker).
- Chains: slot-survival curves and AUC; the share of losses that are reversions to the reproducer's prior vs omissions vs other; tokens spent per retained fact.

**Controls**
- The crossover itself controls general plausibility.
- Position (start, middle, end) is balanced for lost-in-the-middle effects.
- Edit distance and token length of P_A and P_B relative to D are matched and entered as covariates.
- Copies come from a neutral model, so self-preference cannot explain H1. In chains, self-preference is confounded, and that is reported.
- Prompt paraphrases.
- A scorer audit: 200 checker outputs hand-labeled (about an hour of human time) or adjudicated by two models with the disagreements audited.
- Pilot: 100 slots per checker. Final N is set by simulation-based power analysis for OR 1.3 and frozen in the pre-registration before Stage 4.

**Rough cost** (token counts estimated; prices are assumptions, not checked)
- Stage 1: about 28k calls, about 40M input tokens; most on open weights.
- Stage 4: about 6k calls per checker × 7 checkers ≈ 105M input and 10M output tokens; about 45M input on APIs.
- Stage 5: about 30k hops ≈ 150M input and 35M output tokens, mainly open-weight, plus a frontier subset of about 50M input.
- Assuming mid-tier frontier models at a few dollars per million input tokens: about **$0.5k-3k of API spend** plus about **1-3 days on one 8×H100 node** (vLLM) for the open models.

**Agent-buildable steps** (each with an acceptance test)
1. Slot schema and document generator. Test: every slot value appears exactly once and parses.
2. Prior-elicitation module (vLLM logprob scoring plus API sampling). Test: stable modes across two seeds on 50 slots.
3. Divergent-slot selector and D-value chooser. Test: the selected set meets the probability thresholds.
4. Copy generator with a slot-preservation verifier. Test: 100% slot survival in accepted copies.
5. Corruption injector with crossover bookkeeping and grammaticality check.
6. Checker harness with JSON-schema outputs and retries, plus a scorer validated on the audit set (≥ 95% agreement).
7. Chain runner with arms (a)-(e) and full per-hop logs in parquet.
8. Pre-registration document (OSF) generated and code hash frozen before Stage 4. Pilot data excluded from the confirmatory analysis.
9. Analysis scripts and pre-specified figures (interaction plot, dose-response curve, survival curves).

Nate's role: approve the slot taxonomy, review the pre-registration, and spot-check the scorer audit.

**What a positive result would mean**
- LLM verification is not independent error correction when the error agrees with the verifier's priors. Self-review and same-family review give false assurance exactly for "regression to the default" errors: dropped negations, reverted non-standard config values, softened constraints. These are the errors that break long-running agents and safety-relevant memory.
- Design rules that follow:
  - Keep a fixed root.
  - Anchor critical spans mechanically.
  - Choose checkers for *measured prior divergence*, not raw capability.
  - Report checker-prior overlap whenever AI checks AI. This extends Goel et al.'s similarity concern from judging to source-grounded verification.

**What a negative result would mean**
- Source-grounded verification is prior-robust. Compaction drift then has to be fixed at generation time, and the tradition's "the checker must stand outside" claim does not carry over to LLMs.

## Novelty and lab relevance
**Novelty: medium.** The ingredients are published and verified: attractor drift (Perez), failures of self-correction (Huang), judge similarity bias and correlated errors (Goel; Kim), familiar-information bias under conflicting evidence (Xie; ClashEval), and context collapse (ACE). These make *some* prior effect likely. Three things appear to be open:
- the crossover design that pins verification misses on the *checker's own* prior, with the source present;
- the dose-response test;
- the link from that blind spot to compaction-chain retention, compared against mechanical anchors.

That judgment rests on a budget-limited search.

Why a lab would care:
- **Agent reliability:** context compaction and memory files sit on the critical path of long-horizon coding agents.
- **Safety and oversight:** if monitors and graders share the policy's priors, they will under-detect "plausible-looking" deviations. That is the failure mode scalable oversight is meant to prevent, and a prior-overlap metric could predict where it happens.
- **Data:** synthetic-data and self-distillation pipelines that use model checkers would inherit the same blind spot.
- **Cost:** the experiment is cheap, fully automatable and pre-registerable, and returns a design rule whichever way it comes out.

## Where the analogy breaks
- **Exact copying is free for models.** A context window holds the source exactly. Drift happens only when a system *chooses* lossy regeneration. The right human analogue is a Bartlett transmission chain of near-identical heads (Griffiths-Kalish iterated learning with a shared prior), not one person's decaying memory.
- **Traditional checkers shared the performer's priors.** The Roman custos, the brahman priest and co-reciters were insiders. Their power came from a fixed reference and role separation, with sacred stakes behind them, not from cognitive diversity. The "different-family checker" arm is a modern extrapolation. The tradition-faithful arms are the mechanical anchor and root resync, and the Kiddushin 30a lesson warns that even those decay when the checker's reference knowledge drifts.
- **Some apparent "prior" may be a post-training policy.** Softened warnings or added hedges may come from safety tuning, not a memory-like prior. Stage 1 elicits fills as content predictions, but policy effects could still leak into the chains.
- **Route or loci mnemonics do not carry over directly.** They exploit dedicated hippocampal spatial machinery that transformers lack. Structural parallels (Whittington et al. 2022) are suggestive only, which is why the loci and songline hook was not chosen.
- **Fidelity in the traditions also served power and legitimacy:** who controls the canon, who may read the lukasa. Pure accuracy was not the only goal. In some cases, such as the Luba readers and the bhanaka lineages, sanctioned variation was the point.
