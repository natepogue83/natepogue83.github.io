# R11: Words can damage knowing: effortless action, the fish-trap, and when explicit deliberation hurts

## Verdict: weak - The cross-traditional convergence is mostly a modern merger of different claims, with Warring States China the only clear independent source for the craft version, and the psychology survives only as a modest, task-specific effect of explaining rather than of putting things into words as such.

Research note: this session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through (200 searches per turn across all agents), followed by the WebFetch budget. Items I confirmed are marked VERIFIED or "confirmed". Items taken from the theme file that I could not re-check are marked UNVERIFIED. A few points rest on well-known primary texts I could not re-open here, and those are flagged "not re-checked".

## Best defensible version

Some of the knowledge behind skilled judgment, especially perceptual, statistical or configural knowledge, is not fully available in verbal form. Making someone produce an explicit account (an explanation, justification or rule statement, as opposed to a running description of what they are doing) can shift their processing toward whatever is easy to verbalize. That shift reduces performance on tasks that depend on the non-verbal information. In humans the effect is real but modest, depends on the task and on timing, and looks more like a reversible change in processing mode than destruction of the underlying knowledge (Schooler 2002's transfer-inappropriate processing shift).

Of the listed traditions, only the Zhuangzi clearly states the craft version: Cook Ding, Wheelwright Pian, and the fish-trap of ch. 26. The Indian and Greek apophatic material makes a different claim, about the limits of language for an absolute or for the knowing subject. That should be treated as a separate theme, not as corroboration.

On Galton's problem: roughly three roots survive (China; India; Plato), and they make three different claims. The only cross-traditional overlap is the generic theme of the limits of language for the ultimate. Sells (1994) and the constructivist line from Katz (1978) (existence confirmed) explain that overlap without any shared insight about minds: try to name an absolute and you hit the same self-referential paradoxes. The craft observation (novices deliberate, experts flow) is probably universal across craft cultures, which makes its presence in any given tradition weak evidence. There is a further imposition risk: "ineffability" as a defining mark of mysticism is a category from James-era Western scholarship of religion, applied back onto these texts.

## Corrected or overstated claims

1. **"No documented contact" between China, India and Greece.** Overstated for the Greek-Indian pair after Alexander: Pyrrho in India, and Plotinus' reported wish to study Indian philosophy. The Gita is commonly dated after Alexander. No specific influence on the Gita is documented, though.
2. **"They converge on two specific claims."** Overstated. Only the Zhuangzi makes both claims, that grasping degrades action and that craft knowledge is lost in words. The Gita's non-attachment is soteriological. Upanisadic and Neoplatonic apophasis concerns the absolute or the subject, not tacit skill.
3. **"Experimental psychology later found both."** Overstated.
   - Verbal overshadowing survived the registered replication, but at 4% (RRR1, description given immediately) and 16% (RRR2, description delayed) lower lineup accuracy, and the size depends heavily on timing (Alogna et al. 2014, VERIFIED).
   - Verbalization hurting insight problems (Schooler, Ohlsson & Brooks 1993) has a mixed record. Fleck & Weisberg (2004, 2013) found no think-aloud effect. Gilhooly et al. (2010) found no insight-specific effect. Ball et al. (2015) gave complicated results. Macchi & Bagassi (2012) and Macchi et al. (2025) found support (as summarized in Macchi et al. 2025, J. Intelligence, VERIFIED).
   - Reactivity to verbalizing depends on its kind. Fox, Ericsson & Best (2011, Psych. Bull. 137:316-344, VERIFIED) found that plain think-aloud is largely non-reactive, while directed or explanatory verbalization changes performance. So the damage comes from explaining, not from words as such.
4. **"Self-watching ruins skilled performance."** The broader attentional-focus literature behind this, internal versus external focus, may be inflated by reporting bias. A robust Bayesian meta-analysis found bias-corrected effects near zero, g = 0.01 for performance (McKay et al. 2023/2024, sportRxiv preprint, VERIFIED as a preprint; not peer-reviewed as far as I could see). Choking itself is real, but its mechanism (explicit monitoring, distraction or over-arousal) is unresolved, and the neuroimaging leans on reverse inference (Yu 2015 review, VERIFIED).
5. **"Words overwrite tacit representations."** The originator's later account says otherwise. Schooler (2002, Applied Cognitive Psychology 16:989-997, VERIFIED) favours a processing shift. The effect reverses after brief non-verbal tasks and weakens under speeded responding, which suggests the original memory is still accessible.
6. **Liu et al. 2024 figures.**
   - The 36.3-point o1-preview figure is a cross-model comparison against zero-shot GPT-4o on a subset, not a within-model thinking toggle.
   - "Models dropped 11-14 points on faces" holds for GPT-4o (12.8), Claude 3 Opus (14.4) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (11.4). Claude 3.5 Sonnet dropped 3.0, and the two InternVL models dropped about 2-3.
   - GPT-4o's AGL drop of 87.5% to 64.4% and the 331% increase in rounds for exceptions are correct (VERIFIED from paper HTML).
7. **Limb & Braun 2008** had six participants (VERIFIED). It should not be used as corroboration.
8. **The "unconscious thought advantage"** failed: N = 399 replication, and the bias-corrected meta-analytic effect was not significant (Nieuwenstein et al. 2015, VERIFIED). So "deliberation hurts complex choices" is not supported.

## What humans may have been working out about minds

- **Knowing-how outruns telling.** Craft traditions noticed that apprenticeship transmits what manuals cannot. This is plausible and consistent with tacit or implicit learning research. It is also an observation any craft culture would make, so its appearance in religious texts is weak evidence of a deep discovery.
- **Explaining shifts how you process.** Being asked to justify can pull attention onto the verbalizable features and away from configural or statistical information. This is the part best supported by experiment (verbal overshadowing, reactivity of explanation), though the effect is modest.
- **Outcome-grasping disturbs action.** Plausible, as with choking under pressure, but the mechanism is unsettled and the related attentional-focus literature is bias-inflated.
- **The apophatic paradoxes are about logic and reference, not cognition.** "Neti neti" and the One beyond being look more like discoveries about self-reference, a subject that cannot be its own object, than about how minds process information.

## AI problem

When should a reasoning model think, and does its verbal trace carry what it actually knows? Reasoning is now on by default in many deployed models, and several results show it hurting on specific task types:
- implicit statistical learning and verbal-overshadowing analogues (Liu et al.)
- pattern-based in-context learning (Curse of CoT)
- instruction following (Li et al. 2025)
- distractor and spurious-feature tasks (Gema et al.)

The surviving theme points to a specific open question. When thinking hurts, is the model's in-context "tacit" knowledge **destroyed** (overwrite), still present but **outvoted** by the verbal trace at readout (override), or is the damage merely a side effect of **context length** (distance)? The answer matters for:
- adaptive compute: whether to gate thinking before it starts, or rescue the answer after
- CoT monitorability: whether the trace is a complete account of what drives the answer
- capability evaluation: whether evaluations with reasoning on understate tacit capabilities

## How much is already done

Most of it, at the behavioral level.
- **Done:**
  - CoT hurts where verbal thinking hurts humans (Liu et al.).
  - CoT hurts pattern-based ICL, with a distance control (Curse of CoT).
  - Learning or classifying when to think (Thinkless, AdaptThink, Li et al.'s selective reasoning).
  - Overthinking and inverse scaling.
  - Probes that read answer correctness mid-reasoning (Zhang et al., on math).
  - Hidden knowledge exceeding expressed knowledge (Orgad, Gekhman, on QA).
- **Partly done:**
  - Modified classic riddles (idea F).
  - Pressure and evaluation framing (idea A), where EmotionPrompt points the other way and evaluation-awareness work exists but does not cross it with thinking on implicit tasks.
- **Apparently not done** (within my limited search; novelty is provisional because the search budget ran out): a mechanistic test on the verbal-overshadowing task family of **whether thinking overwrites, overrides, or merely distances** the model's in-context knowledge. This means probing internal states under a same-weights thinking toggle, with controls that separate the content of the trace from its length, and with explicit-rule tasks as a crossed control.

## Proposed experiment

**Title: "The Wheelwright Probe": when thinking hurts, does it erase what the model knows or outvote it?**

**Hypotheses (pre-registered).** Each task family (implicit vs explicit) is crossed with thinking (off vs on) and readout (the model's output vs a linear probe on the residual stream at the answer position).

- **H1 (behavioral replication in open models).** On implicit-statistical in-context tasks, thinking-on output accuracy is below thinking-off. On format-matched explicit-rule tasks, it is at or above thinking-off. This is a family × thinking interaction.
- **H2-override (tradition-derived, following Schooler's TIPS account).** The words do not carry the knack but do not destroy it. On implicit tasks with thinking on, probe accuracy stays near thinking-off levels while output accuracy falls. The latent-overt gap (probe accuracy minus output accuracy) therefore grows with thinking, more for implicit than explicit tasks, and more with the model's own relevant trace than with length-matched irrelevant text. **Primary test:** the gap × thinking × family interaction is greater than 0.
- **H2-overwrite (strong "words damage knowing").** The probe falls in step with the output, so the gap is unchanged. Tested for equivalence with TOST, bound ±3 points.
- **H2-distance (Curse-of-CoT style).** The same pattern appears with length-matched filler or another item's trace, so verbal content is not the cause.
- **Default ML intuition (H0).** Thinking is neutral or helpful in open models, or else readout and output move together, because the answer-position residual is what produces the logit.

**Setup.**
- **Tasks.** Six text-only tasks, each with a fresh random structure per prompt, so that a probe cannot learn the labels from surface features shared across items.
  - **Implicit family:**
    - T1: Reber-style artificial grammar, with a new random finite-state grammar per prompt, 25 grammatical strings in context, and grammatical vs. ungrammatical test strings.
    - T2: prototype-distortion categorization, with two random symbol-string prototypes, exemplars made by k substitutions, and new distortions at test.
    - T3: a rule-plus-exceptions classifier with about 20% memorized exceptions (a single-turn analogue of Liu et al.'s task).
  - **Explicit family** (same surface formats):
    - C1: a compact conjunctive feature rule in T2's format.
    - C2: a short deterministic string rule in T1's format.
    - C3: a modular mapping in ICL format.
  - In a pilot, tune difficulty so thinking-off accuracy is 70-85% for every task and model. This keeps ceiling and floor effects from driving the interaction.
- **Conditions per item:**
  1. thinking off
  2. thinking on (natural length)
  3. thinking on at forced budgets of about 256, 1k and 4k tokens (budget forcing)
  4. length-matched filler (the model's own neutral recitation, following Curse of CoT)
  5. swapped trace (the model's own trace from a different item of the same task, which is fluent and on-genre but irrelevant)
  6. early-answer checkpoints at 10%, 20% ... 100% of the trace: append the end-of-thinking token plus "Answer:" and read out both the output and the probe.
- **Causal arm (secondary).** Knock out attention from the answer position to the thinking tokens (from layer L onward, with L swept). Override predicts that thinking-off accuracy is recovered. Overwrite predicts no recovery, because the damage has already propagated through the demonstration and trace representations.

**Models.**
- **Primary:** open-weight models whose thinking can be toggled on the same weights, e.g. the Qwen3 family (4B/8B/14B/32B, enable_thinking flag) and gpt-oss-20b (reasoning effort low vs high).
- **Secondary:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill models using the NoThinking prompt from Ma et al.
- **Behavioral-only arm:** two or three current frontier API models with a thinking toggle or budget (Claude, GPT and Gemini families), to check that H1 generalizes.

**Metrics.**
- Output accuracy, scored from the log-probabilities of the two label tokens, which avoids parsing.
- Probe accuracy: logistic regression on the residual at the answer position, per layer. Use grouped 5-fold CV by structure seed, with the layer chosen on a held-out dev split by a pre-registered rule.
- The latent-overt gap, and the gap trajectory over early-answer checkpoints.
- Probe transfer: train on thinking-off runs and test on thinking-on runs, as a second readout.
- Capture index (secondary): ask the model to state its rule as an executable predicate, then measure P(output follows the stated rule | stated rule disagrees with ground truth).

**Controls.**
1. Hewitt & Liang control tasks (labels permuted within seed) to report probe selectivity.
2. A mismatched-context probe (same test string, demos from a different structure), which must fall to chance. This shows the probe reads in-context knowledge rather than string features.
3. A surface-feature baseline: an n-gram classifier trained across seeds must be at chance on held-out seeds.
4. Filler and swapped-trace conditions to separate content from distance.
5. The explicit-rule family as the crossed control.
6. Equal item sets across conditions, with paired analysis.

**Statistics.** Mixed-effects logistic regression: correct ~ thinking × family × readout + (1|seed) + (1|task) + (1|model). Pre-register the primary interaction, the TOST bounds for overwrite, and the exclusion rules: drop items whose trace is truncated, and drop models whose pilot accuracy is outside 70-85%. Plan about 1,000 prompt instances per task per model, with a fresh structure each. That gives a standard error of about 1.4 points per cell and about 80% power for an interaction of around 6 points. Re-estimate after the pilot.

**Cost (rough estimate).**
- Open models: about 6,000 prompts × 7 conditions per model. Thinking traces average about 2k tokens, and the filler, swapped-trace and checkpoint conditions need forward passes only. Roughly 20-60 H100-hours in total across 3-4 models, about $50-200 at typical cloud rates.
- Frontier API arm: about 9,000 thinking calls × about 2k output tokens, around 18M output tokens, roughly $200-1,000 depending on the model tier.
- Total: about $300-1,500, plus agent time.

**Agent-buildable steps.**
1. Task-generator package (pure Python, seeded, with difficulty knobs), plus unit tests for label correctness and the surface-feature-baseline-at-chance check.
2. A pilot that tunes difficulty per model, followed by pre-registration (OSF) before the main run.
3. A vLLM generation harness (chat templates with thinking toggles, budget forcing) that writes JSONL traces.
4. An HF-transformers/nnsight harness that caches the residual at the answer position for every condition and checkpoint, including filler, swapped-trace and knockout masks (a custom 4D attention mask).
5. Probe training and selectivity controls (scikit-learn, grouped CV).
6. The frontier API behavioral arm.
7. The pre-registered mixed-effects and TOST analysis (statsmodels), plus plots of output and probe accuracy against thinking length by family.

Each step has a machine-checkable test, so a coding agent can build and verify it without hand-coding.

**Meaning of the possible results.**
- **Override.** On these tasks the model still "knows" the answer after thinking, but the verbal trace outvotes it at readout. Practical consequences:
  - A cheap probe could rescue answers after thinking: let the latent answer vote whenever probe and output disagree on implicit-type inputs.
  - Evaluations run with reasoning on may understate tacit capabilities.
  - On these tasks the trace is causally load-bearing (good for monitorability) but is an incomplete account of what the model knows.
- **Overwrite.** Thinking degrades the in-context representation itself. Gating has to happen before thinking starts, and RL-trained reasoning may be washing out non-verbal competence.
- **Distance.** The effect comes from context length, not from verbal content. For models, the tradition-derived mechanism is refuted.

Each outcome is informative, and only override vindicates the "fish-trap" reading (discard the words at the moment of answering).

## Novelty and lab relevance

**Novelty: medium.**
- Already done: the behavioral phenomenon (Liu et al.; Curse of CoT, including a distance control), when-to-think routing, probing for correctness during reasoning, and hidden knowledge.
- What appears new is combining them into a three-way mechanistic discrimination (override, overwrite, distance) on the human verbal-overshadowing task family, using a same-weights thinking toggle, an own-versus-swapped-trace control, and an attention-knockout test.
- That judgment rests on a search that was cut short and should be re-checked before investing.

**Why labs would care:**
- Capabilities and inference: reasoning is on by default, and allocating test-time compute needs to know whether thinking damage can be undone after the fact.
- Safety: CoT monitorability (Korbak et al.) assumes the trace reflects what drives behavior. A systematic latent-overt gap that thinking creates is a measurable kind of incompleteness, separate from deliberate unfaithfulness.
- Evaluations: elicitation protocols with reasoning on might under-measure some capabilities.

## Where the analogy breaks

- **Tokens are both report and working memory.** In a transformer, the verbal trace is the computation's own scratchpad. In humans, verbal report sits on top of separate non-verbal systems. "Overwriting" means something different in each.
- **Nothing decays in context.** The demonstrations remain fully available, whereas human verbal overshadowing concerns a fading memory trace. Model overwrite can only mean a change in attention or representation, not lost storage.
- **No arousal or self-consciousness.** "Choking" prompts change the input distribution and nothing more, so idea A has no real mechanistic analogue (and EmotionPrompt shows the opposite sign).
- **Reasoning traces are RL-shaped policy outputs, not introspection.** Their link to internal computation is trained, not evolved.
- **Linear decodability is not use.** A probe showing the answer is present does not show the model "knows" it in the craftsman's sense. That is why the knockout arm is needed.
- **Human skill takes years to proceduralize; in-context "knowledge" forms in one forward pass.**
- **The religious material inspires the hypothesis but does not support it.** Apophasis about the absolute does not bear on the experiment at all. Only the Zhuangzi craft strand and Schooler's psychology do.
